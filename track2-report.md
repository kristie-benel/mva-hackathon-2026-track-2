## Summary

Mosaic variegated aneuploidy (MVA) is a *BUB1B* genetic disorder in which the BUBR1 protein degrades too quickly, causing mistakes in the cell's spindle checkpoint. We propose two small-molecule drugs that act on this problem: nicotinamide riboside, which boosts NAD+, and a CBP/p300 inhibitor (inobrodib), which reduces protein acetylation. Both are intended to stabilize BUBR1 and slow its degradation. They could eventually be combined into a single treatment plan. We recommend testing the proposal in mouse and *C. elegans* worm models.

## 1. Overview of the Disorder

MVA is one of the rarest genetic disorders in the world, with only about 50 known cases. It is caused by a dysregulated cell cycle that leads to an incorrect number of chromosomes in some cells. This produces a variety of symptoms, including slow growth, low muscle tone, developmental delays, and increased cancer risk.

The disorder is linked to the *BUB1B* gene, which codes for the BUBR1 protein. BUBR1 is an enzyme that regulates the mitotic spindle checkpoint, the cell's quality-control step that makes sure chromosomes are correctly attached before a cell divides.

## 2. Involved Biological Mechanisms

BUBR1 regulates the mitotic spindle checkpoint, spindle assembly on chromosomes, and kinetochore-microtubule interactions, and it ensures proper chromosome segregation. When there is a fault in the *BUB1B* gene, cells do not segregate their chromosomes properly, and some cells become aneuploid. This can lead to a wide variety of symptoms and congenital abnormalities.

## 3. Variant Mechanism

The variant mechanism describes the precise molecular consequence of a specific genetic alteration on a gene, transcript, or protein. In this case, the BUBR1 protein degrades too quickly, which causes mistakes in the spindle checkpoint.

This is a loss-of-function mechanism: too little working BUBR1 remains in the cell because the protein is lost too quickly. The pathway disrupted is the mitotic spindle checkpoint, and the downstream biological consequence is incorrect chromosome segregation, which produces aneuploid cells and the symptoms described above.

Our proposed treatment will boost NAD+ and suppress protein acetylation. Together, these changes are expected to stabilize the protein and slow its degradation.

## 4. Chosen Drugs

We propose two drugs, both of which are small molecules.

**Nicotinamide riboside.** This is a form of vitamin B3 that is approved as a dietary supplement. It boosts NAD+. The commercial form is widely available and is marketed as Tru Niagen.

**CBP/p300 inhibitors (inobrodib).** These drugs reduce protein acetylation by inhibiting the CBP/p300 enzymes. Inobrodib is not on the market yet, but it has been given Orphan Drug and Fast Track status by the FDA, and it is slated to be approved by 2029.

Eventually, these two treatments could be combined to boost NAD+ and reduce protein acetylation at the same time.

### Reasoning and biochemistry

The two drugs attack the same problem from opposite sides. NAD+ is a cofactor for enzymes that remove acetyl groups from proteins, so raising NAD+ with nicotinamide riboside favors deacetylation. CBP/p300 are enzymes that add acetyl groups to proteins, so inhibiting them reduces acetylation directly. Because acetylation can mark a protein for degradation, lowering it is expected to help BUBR1 persist longer in the cell. Used together, the drugs could push BUBR1 toward a more stable state.

## 5. Repurposing the Chosen Drugs

The molecules being applied to treat MVA are NAD+ (supplied through nicotinamide riboside) and the inobrodib molecule. Both relate directly back to the variant mechanism, because they are able to stabilize the affected protein.

## 6. Limitations and Implications

### Limitations

Neither drug has been tested for MVA, and this proposal is based on biological reasoning rather than laboratory or patient data. Because MVA is so rare, evidence about how these drugs would behave in affected children is not available.

### Risks

No risks have been identified with this specific use, because these specific drugs have not been tested for MVA. This reflects the lack of testing and should not be read as proof of safety.

### Ethical concerns

We are dedicated to advancing human health through the responsible scientific exploration of repurposed therapeutics. We recognize that re-evaluating established biochemical compounds offers an efficient pathway to treatment of unmet medical needs; nevertheless, it demands strict adherence to ethical research standards. Voluntary informed consent remains paramount, as we advocate for affordable global access to effective repurposed therapies. All activities should also align with applicable national and international regulatory standards and prioritize rigorous pharmacovigilance.

### Implications

- **Research goals:** This work contributes to the current research goals of the MVA Society, which is to stabilize the BUBR1 protein.
- **Diagnosis:** By focusing on why BUBR1 is unstable, this work may also help clarify how the disorder arises, which could inform future diagnosis of MVA.
- **Scalability:** Nicotinamide riboside is widely available and easily accessible to the public.

## 7. Discussion and Recommendations

We recommend that our proposed treatment be tested in mouse and *C. elegans* worm models. We also recommend synthesizing both drugs as part of a larger treatment plan, so that NAD+ levels and protein acetylation can be addressed together.
